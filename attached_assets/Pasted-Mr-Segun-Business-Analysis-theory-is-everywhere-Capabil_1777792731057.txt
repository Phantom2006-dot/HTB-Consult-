Mr Segun: Business Analysis theory is everywhere. Capability is not.
I have seen this play out more times than I can count.
Someone takes a course. They learn the terms. They understand what a Business Analyst is supposed to do. On paper, everything looks right.
Then they step into a real project.
The conversations feel different. The questions are not as straightforward. Stakeholders are not aligned. Nothing is clearly defined. And slowly, that confidence starts to shake.
Not because they are not capable, but because the foundation was not built in the right order.
This is where a simple idea becomes important.
Mindset before skillset.
Many people approach skill acquisition with one goal in mind, getting into the room.
Get the knowledge. Get the role. Get the opportunity.
But very few stop to ask a more important question.
What does it take to stay in the room?
Because getting in is one thing. Contributing meaningfully once you are there is something else entirely.
In real environments, nobody hands you a clean problem statement. You are expected to make sense of things that are still forming. You are expected to ask questions that others have not thought to ask. You are expected to bring structure where there is none.
That is the real work of Business Analysis.
It is not just about documenting requirements or following a process. It is about thinking clearly in situations that are not yet clear.
And this is where mindset comes first.
If your mindset is built around quick wins, surface understanding, or simply ticking boxes, you will struggle when things become uncertain.
But if your mindset is built around curiosity, patience, and a genuine desire to understand how things work, your skillset begins to develop differently.
You start to listen more intentionally.
You begin to question more thoughtfully.
You become comfortable working through ambiguity instead of avoiding it.
And over time, your skills stop being theoretical. They become practical.
When the focus is only on skill acquisition, the foundation can become shallow. But when the mindset is right, the skills grow with depth and context.
That is what allows someone not just to enter the room, but to remain relevant within it.
Real skill development is not rushed.
It requires structure. It requires reflection. It requires the discipline to go beyond surface level learning and engage with how things actually work in practice.
Because over time, knowledge becomes judgement.
And judgement is what allows you to operate with confidence, even when things are not perfect.
The difference is not in how much you know. It is in how you think, how you approach problems, and how you respond when things are not clearly defined.
That is why mindset comes first.
Because when the mindset is right, the skillset has somewhere solid to grow.
That is where real capability is built.
And that is where real Business Analysis begins.
